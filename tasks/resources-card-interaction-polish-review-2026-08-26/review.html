What changed, and why

This is an interaction hierarchy correction, not a removal of feedback. Focus rings and 0.96 press feedback remain on the existing actions; reference shortcuts still have a subtle action hover.

Guide cards carried too much secondary content

Was

Each guide card surfaced a Markdown preview, visibility/audience labels, author, and date alongside the title, making the library feel closer to a content feed than a quick-reference index.

Now

Web guide cards and the native iOS Guides row are title-first surfaces. Small type/status cues remain for orientation, while preview text, visibility/audience labels, author names, and dates are left to the guide reader.

Every guide card announced hover at the container level

Was

Guide cards and list rows changed border color and added a shadow whenever the pointer entered any part of the link, making every library tile feel like a raised control.

Now

Guide surfaces keep their border and shadow quiet on hover. The existing link remains fully interactive and adds a restrained chevron cue near the title.

Non-actionable contact cards implied a whole-card action

Was

Contact cards used the same border/shadow hover treatment even though the card itself is a layout container around individual contact links and a View profile action.

Now

Contact cards are visually quiet; the individual fields and profile action retain their own interaction targets.

The remaining card cues needed a quieter hierarchy

Was

Type icons and small status/category cues blended into the card content, while the broad hover state carried too much of the visual hierarchy.

Now

Type icons use a quiet bordered treatment, small type/status cues stay compact, and the guide title receives a little more identity without adding a new semantic color.
