Heading utilities were inert

Chasing dead heading classes turned up the reason they were dead: the typography block in globals.css sits outside any @layer, so unlayered CSS outranks every Tailwind utility. 141 heading declarations across the app silently do nothing — and the public marketing hero was rendering at a third of its authored size.

What changed, and why

The root cause was diagnosed, measured, and then deliberately left unfixed. Only the demonstrable bug was shipped.

The cascade, measured rather than assumed

Was

I had already written into the design language that heading utilities are dead code. That claim predicted the /about hero would render at 30px, which would look broken — so it was worth testing rather than trusting.

Now

Confirmed in the browser: on an h1, text-5xl, text-[32px], and a bare element all compute to 30px. The typography block opens after the @layer base block closes, and unlayered CSS outranks every layered rule. The claim held, and the hero really was broken.

The public hero was fixed narrowly

Was

Five showroom headings carried authored sizes and weights that never applied.

Now

They use the Tailwind important modifier (text-5xl!, font-black!). Five call sites, zero effect anywhere else — verified by measuring /login and /support before and after.

The correct architectural fix was measured and not shipped

Was

Moving the typography block into @layer base makes utilities behave normally and is the right structure.

Now

Tried, measured, reverted. It changes 141 headings at once — 59 CardTitles, 46 h3s, 27 h2s, 6 h1s, 3 dialog and sheet titles — most on authenticated surfaces that cannot be verified locally. Shipping that as a side effect of a heading cleanup would have been reckless. It is written up in docs/DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md with the measurement and the impact count, and queued in the conformance checklist as its own slice.

Inert declarations were removed rather than left to mislead

Was

/support, /privacy, and the 404 page carried text-4xl, text-2xl, and font-semibold on headings that ignored them.

Now

Removed. Seven declarations, all provably no-ops, so the rendering is byte-identical and the next reader is not misled into thinking the scale is set locally.

Verification

  • TypeScript: clean. Lint: clean across src/app, src/components, and tests.
  • Full suite: 3615 passing, 0 failing, 536 files.
  • Build: npm run build:app compiles.
  • Docs: npm run verify:docs reports codemaps current.
  • Scope proof: /login and /support h1 measured at 30px/800 both before and after, confirming the showroom fix did not leak into the app shells.
  • Cascade proof: three synthetic h1 elements injected into the live page returned identical computed sizes, isolating the behaviour from any one page's markup.

What this does not prove

  • The 141-site fix is untested. It was applied, measured on three public routes, then reverted. No authenticated surface was checked while it was in place, which is exactly why it was not kept.
  • Only three public routes were captured. /about/tech-stack, /about/security, and /about/field-work share the same showroom blocks and were not individually captured.
  • Mobile widths were not captured. The showroom headings are responsive (text-5xl base, sm:text-7xl, lg:text-8xl); only the 1280px case was verified visually.
  • Related open item: Input and SelectTrigger are h-9 (36px), below the 40px target rule. FormCombobox was left at h-9 to stay aligned with them during the target sweep; raising all three together needs its own slice.
